# Customer Audit Report

> User Level: Customer - Investigation #9

## Summary

The Customer has access to 7 modules — all under Customer Portal. Compared to the CEO (23 staff modules) or Account Manager (19), this set is deliberately narrow: browse and discover vehicles, talk to staff, and manage buying, selling, and special requests. This focus is correct. The Customer's job is to find a car, ask questions, and complete a purchase or sale — not to handle internal money, payroll, inspections, or staff management — so the system keeps the role on showroom, personal requests, and personal records while blocking all staff operations.

All 7 modules are necessary for a complete buy/sell experience; none should be removed. Five are core working tools that should stay as they are. Two request flows (Request a Car, Sell Vehicle) are already simple but benefit from small wording and guidance improvements. The only area that needs simplification is Transactions, where a purchase involves several steps that should be shown as a clear progress guide.

Sidebar order is Customer Portal (Showroom → Find Your Car → My Inquiries → Transactions → Request a Car → Sell Vehicle → Favourites). The configured landing page after sign-in is `/showroom`, which matches browsing-first behavior.

## Modules Audited

### 1. Showroom — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** The main browsing page. Shows available and reserved vehicles in a card grid with photo placeholder, make/model/year, price, pricing type (fixed/negotiable), mileage/fuel/transmission, and any offer badge. Supports searching (make/model/stock code), filtering by make, sorting (Newest, Price Low→High, Price High→Low), and saving a car with the heart button. Each card opens a detail page with photos/360-views, full specs (condition, mileage, transmission, fuel, body, engine, color, stock code), price and offer, warranty, description, and "Inquiry/Buy" buttons.
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — this is the entry point. Without it the Customer cannot see what is for sale, compare options, or start a conversation.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — like a normal online shop. Search and filters work on the page without reloading, and the vehicle count updates live. The grid handles empty results with a friendly message. Detail page loads only public media and shows one clear action area at the bottom.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve as the landing workspace. No broader permission needed; adding recently-viewed or "back to results" on the detail page would be a small convenience, not a required change.

### 2. Find Your Car (Recommendations) — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** A guided helper to narrow choices. The Customer enters a budget (slider ₱50k–₱5M plus number input) and optional preferences — condition (any/new/certified/used), fuel (any/electric/hybrid/diesel/petrol), make, and body type — then taps "Get Recommendations" to receive a ranked list of available vehicles scored on five weighted criteria (shown as badges with percentages).
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — helps a Customer who knows a budget but not which car fits best. It complements Showroom browsing rather than replacing it; the Showroom also links directly to this page with "Find Your Car."
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — single short form, one submit, toast confirmation, then results view. Fields are optional except budget, so the Customer can start broad and refine. The scoring badges are visible before submitting so expectations are clear.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep as is. Consider simplifying the wording around the five scoring criteria (show a one-line plain explanation of what each means) so a non-technical Customer understands why a car ranked higher, with full scoring detail on drill-down.

### 3. My Inquiries — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** The Customer's conversation inbox. Lists the Customer's own inquiries (vehicle, state, update time) with unread counts for new staff replies, and opens a chat thread with message history, attachments, and any scheduled viewing arrangement. The Customer can send messages and attach a file in the same thread.
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — core to asking about a vehicle, negotiating, and scheduling a viewing without calling or emailing outside the system.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — familiar chat layout (list on left, conversation on right, back button on mobile). Messages are ordered by time, unread is tracked per inquiry, and file sharing uses a simple upload. Creating an inquiry starts from the Showroom detail (Inquiry/Buy buttons), so there is no manual form to fill.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve. No simplification needed; the empty state guides the Customer ("Browse the showroom to start one") and the thread loads only the Customer's own data, which is correct for privacy.

### 4. Transactions — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** The Customer's purchase and sale history in one place. Shows all transactions the Customer created — buy (purchase), sell (sell vehicle), and request-a-car — with vehicle label, stock code, kind, status (pending, under review, approved, etc.), and opened date. Supports search, kind filter, status filter, sorting, and pagination. Each row opens a detail/print view with price, payment method, arrangement (delivery/meetup/GCE visit with schedule/location), and document uploads (valid ID, proof of billing).
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — the Customer needs one place to track what they asked for, what they are buying or selling, and what still needs action (e.g., upload ID, confirm arrangement, cancel if needed).
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for tracking, but potentially complex for a first-time buyer. A purchase can involve several steps after the initial request: choosing payment method and final price, scheduling a viewing/delivery, uploading ID and billing proof, and waiting for staff review. The table itself is clear (toggles and filters are small and on top), but the multi-step detail flow is not shown as a single progress guide on the Customer side.
- **Recommendation:** Simplify — keep the list and filters as they are, but add a simple progress stepper at the top of the detail page (e.g., Requested → Details → Documents → Review → Complete/Cancel) and plain-language status text so the Customer always knows the next step. No module removal needed; the underlying steps and file upload for purchases are correct and should be preserved.

### 5. Request a Car — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** A short form to ask the dealership to find a specific car that is not in the Showroom. The Customer enters make and model (required), optional year range (min/max), budget (required, in pesos), and other preferences (color, transmission, fuel, etc., in a free-text box), then submits. The request creates a "request_a_car" transaction and appears in Transactions with a link to its detail.
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — covers the common case where the Customer wants something not currently listed. Without it the only option is to keep browsing or ask via chat without structured detail.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — concise. Five required/optional fields plus one free-text area, with validation (e.g., min year cannot be greater than max year, budget cannot be negative). Submit shows a success message and jumps to the new transaction detail. Cancel returns to the previous page.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep the simple form. Consider slightly plainer help text under Budget ("Maximum you are willing to pay") and under Year range ("Leave blank if flexible") so a non-technical Customer does not hesitate over optional fields.

### 6. Sell Vehicle — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** A short form to offer the Customer's own car to the dealership for evaluation. The Customer enters make, model, year, mileage (km), condition (free-text), offered amount in pesos, and optional description. Submitting creates a "sell" transaction with a draft vehicle record (stock code like SELL-TOY-...) and opens the transaction detail for staff review (mechanic inspection and sales review follow on the staff side).
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — gives the Customer a direct selling channel instead of only buying. It mirrors Request a Car but for the opposite direction (Customer → dealership).
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — single page, six required fields plus description, with numeric validation (year 1900–2100, mileage/amount not negative). Success message and redirect to the transaction detail confirm the submission. No photos are requested on this form, which keeps it light.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep as is. Consider replacing the free-text Condition with a small dropdown (Excellent / Good / Fair / Needs Repair) plus an optional detail box, so Customers describe condition in the same terms staff use, reducing back-and-forth. This is a wording improvement, not a new workflow.

### 7. Favourites — Customer Portal

- **What it does for Customer:** The Customer's shortlist. Shows all vehicles the Customer saved with the heart button in Showroom (or detail page) in the same card grid, with a header count ("You have 3 saved vehicles" or "You haven't saved any yet"). Uses the same search, filter, and sort-friendly grid as Showroom.
- **Necessary for Customer?** Yes — lets the Customer bookmark cars while comparing budgets or waiting for a recommendation, and return later without searching again.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — one-click save/unsave with immediate visual feedback (filled heart) and toast on error. No extra steps; removing a favourite is the same heart tap. Empty state is clear and not an error.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve. No simplification needed; if anything, a small "Clear all" or "Share list" would be a convenience, not a required change.

## Overall Recommendations

- **Keep as is:** Showroom (browsing-first landing), Find Your Car / Recommendations (guided ranking), My Inquiries (chat with staff), Request a Car (short sourcing form), Sell Vehicle (short selling form), Favourites (saved shortlist)
- **Simplify (show summary first, details on drill-down):** Transactions — keep the list, kind/status filters, and search, but add a plain-language progress stepper on the detail page so the multi-step purchase (details → arrangement → documents → review) is clear to a non-technical Customer; keep the existing document upload and cancel controls behind that guide. Find Your Car — add one-line plain explanations for the five scoring badges while keeping the technical percentages on drill-down.
- **Consider for Revision/Removal:** No module is recommended for removal. All seven match the Customer's scope — browsing, buying, selling, inquiries, transactions, and saving. Any fewer would break the core journey (without Showroom or Favourites there is no way to find and compare; without My Inquiries and Transactions there is no way to talk to staff or track purchases; without Request a Car and Sell Vehicle there is no channel for off-list needs). All suggestions preserve existing controls and require no deletion of functionality.

## Notes

- All recommendations are suggestions only — no functionality has been removed. Controls such as customer-only inquiry and transaction scoping (`customer_id = auth user`), document upload restricted to buy transactions (valid ID with required ID type, proof of billing), and staff-only review/approval should be preserved when simplifying views.
- Why the Customer does NOT have staff modules — correct separation: The Customer is intentionally not given Dashboards, Operations, Staff Management, or Admin modules that belong to staff. Modules not assigned are appropriately absent: Finance, Vehicles (staff inventory create/edit/price propose/publish/archive/delete), Staff Showroom editing, Content (banners/promotions), Inspections (mechanic checklist), Inquiries/Recommendations/Transactions (staff coordination views), Roadmap (strategy), Suppliers and Supplier Messages (supplier onboarding and negotiation), Staff Records, Attendance, Employee Requests, Payroll, Payslips, Field Cases, Security Duty Checks, Reports, Announcements creation, Roles, and Users. The Customer should not have access to money movement (Finance, Payroll, Payslips, Reports), internal vehicle management (staff Vehicles, Inspections, Content publish, Roadmap), people management (Staff Records, Attendance, Employee Requests), field/security work (Field Cases, Security Duty Checks), or supplier decisions (Suppliers, Supplier Messages) — that separation keeps financial, HR, and supplier data private, prevents Customers from seeing or editing internal pricing approvals and staff pay, and limits the Customer to what they need: browsing, asking, and transacting on their own behalf.
- Workflow complexity for Customers: No form is overly complex on its own. The only area that may feel heavy is Transactions → buy detail, where the Customer must complete several pieces over time (payment details, arrangement schedule/location/notes, ID and billing documents) before staff can complete the sale. This is a necessary flow for a high-value purchase, but the Customer view should present it as one continuous checklist rather than separate actions scattered on the page. Request a Car and Sell Vehicle are correctly short (5–6 fields) — the only small improvement is plainer help text and, for Sell Vehicle, a dropdown for condition to reduce free-text variation.
