# CEO Audit Report

> User Level: CEO (Chief Executive Officer) - Investigation #1

## Summary

The CEO currently has the broadest access in the system — 23 staff modules covering dashboards, operations, staff management, and administration. This breadth is appropriate for final oversight and accountability, but day-to-day work in several modules is better handled by specialist roles while the CEO keeps a clear summary and approval view. Most modules are necessary; a small number should be simplified to a review-only or summary view rather than removed.

## Modules Audited

### 1. Dashboard (Default) — Dashboards
- **What it does for CEO:** Gives a quick overview of business health — sales, inventory, and open alerts in one place.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO needs a single snapshot to start the day and spot issues.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — read-only overview, no extra steps.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve as the CEO's starting view.

### 2. Finance — Dashboards
- **What it does for CEO:** Shows money in and out, including financial entries and payouts.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO is responsible for financial oversight and sign-off.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — detailed, but detail is needed for control. Could feel heavy day-to-day.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — add a simplified CEO summary at the top while keeping full detail available on request.

### 3. Vehicles — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Shows the full vehicle inventory, condition, pricing, and approval status.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — vehicles are the core product; the CEO must see stock and pricing.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — supports review and approval without forcing the CEO to do data entry.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep approval controls; leave listing and editing to operations staff.

### 4. Showroom (Staff Showroom) — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Shows what customers see when browsing cars, so the CEO can check presentation.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — helps the CEO verify how the business appears to the public.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — viewing and checking, not complex.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — view-only is enough for the CEO; editing can stay with marketing and sales.

### 5. Content — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Stores marketing content such as banners and showroom information.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes, for oversight — but not for daily creation.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for marketing staff; overly hands-on if the CEO is expected to create or publish content.
- **Recommendation:** Simplify — keep a review and approve view for the CEO; leave creation and publishing to Marketing Specialist.

### 6. Inspections — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Tracks vehicle inspections and quality checks before sale.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO needs confidence that cars meet quality standards.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — status tracking with clear outcomes.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep CEO visibility and final sign-off where needed.

### 7. Inquiries — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Shows customer questions and conversations with staff.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes, for insight into customer needs and service quality.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for monitoring; overly complex if the CEO is expected to handle chats directly.
- **Recommendation:** Simplify — keep a summary and monitoring view for the CEO; route daily replies to Sales and Account teams.

### 8. Recommendations — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Presents curated vehicle suggestions and sales overviews for customers.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — helps the CEO understand sales focus and inventory movement.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — overview and allocation view.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve as an oversight tool, not a daily task for the CEO.

### 9. Transactions — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Tracks all sales and payment records in one place.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — central to revenue and accountability.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — filtering and detail are needed for audit.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep full history and controls; consider a one-page CEO summary of totals and exceptions.

### 10. Roadmap — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Lays out plans, projects, and upcoming milestones.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO owns strategy and future direction.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — the CEO is the primary planner, so edit access is correct here.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep CEO as owner; others view as needed.

### 11. Suppliers — Operations
- **What it does for CEO:** Manages supplier records, documents, and approval status.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — supplier quality directly affects inventory and reputation.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — invite, review documents, approve or reject.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve approval control; let Account Manager handle day-to-day collection and follow-up.

### 12. Staff Records — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Holds staff profiles and performance information.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO is responsible for people and organization.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — viewing and reviewing, with sensitive actions protected.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep access; ensure only summary performance data is shown by default.

### 13. Attendance — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Shows who is present, late, or absent.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes, for oversight of staffing and reliability.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for managers; too detailed if the CEO must review every clock-in.
- **Recommendation:** Simplify — show a daily summary and exceptions to the CEO; keep detailed logs with managers and head of security.

### 14. Employee Requests — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Handles staff requests such as leave and other formal requests.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO is a final approver for sensitive requests.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — request, review, approve or reject.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep approval role; filter so only items needing CEO decision reach the CEO.

### 15. Payroll — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Manages payroll runs and total compensation before payout.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — payroll is a major cost and control point.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — review totals and approve runs, not enter every line item.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep approval and summary view; let Account Manager and Head Accountant prepare details.

### 16. Payslips — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Shows individual payslips for each employee.
- **Necessary for CEO?** No — individual slips are not needed for day-to-day CEO decisions; payroll summary is sufficient.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for payroll staff, but overly granular for the CEO.
- **Recommendation:** Consider for Revision — limit CEO to summary and exception access; keep detailed payslip access with Finance. Retain access only when audit requires it.

### 17. Field Cases — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Tracks issues that happen outside the office, such as repossessions or field investigations.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — these cases carry financial and reputational risk.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — assignment and status tracking.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep CEO visibility and final decision where risk is high.

### 18. Security Duty Checks — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Records security rounds and guard check-ins.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes, for safety oversight — but only at summary level.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for security team; too operational if the CEO must review every check.
- **Recommendation:** Simplify — give the CEO a daily compliance summary and missed-check alerts; leave detailed review to Head of Security.

### 19. Reports — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Stores formal business reports for review.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — reports support strategic decisions.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — create, review, and archive.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep as the CEO's source of record for performance and compliance.

### 20. Announcements — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Sends company-wide messages to all staff.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO needs a direct channel to communicate direction and policy.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — simple create and publish.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — preserve, ideally with a review step before broad announcements.

### 21. Supplier Messages — Staff Management
- **What it does for CEO:** Holds direct message threads with suppliers.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes, for relationship oversight and sensitive negotiations.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate for monitoring; direct daily messaging is better handled by responsible managers.
- **Recommendation:** Simplify — keep CEO able to view and step in when needed; assign routine messages to Account Manager or Sales.

### 22. Roles — Pages / Admin
- **What it does for CEO:** Controls what each role in the system is allowed to see and do.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — access control is a core CEO responsibility.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — protected changes with clear ownership.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep CEO as owner of role changes, with audit trail.

### 23. Users — Pages / Admin
- **What it does for CEO:** Manages user accounts — inviting, activating, suspending, and assigning roles.
- **Necessary for CEO?** Yes — the CEO is accountable for who has access.
- **Workflow:** Appropriate — centralized user control.
- **Recommendation:** Keep — keep CEO access; delegate routine invites to Account Manager while CEO retains approval for sensitive changes.

## Overall Recommendations

- **Keep as is:** Dashboard, Vehicles, Showroom, Inspections, Recommendations, Transactions, Roadmap, Suppliers, Staff Records, Employee Requests, Payroll, Field Cases, Reports, Announcements, Roles, Users, Finance (with summary enhancement)
- **Simplify:** Content (CEO to review/approve only), Inquiries (monitor, not reply), Finance and Transactions (add one-page CEO summaries), Attendance (summary and exceptions), Security Duty Checks (daily compliance summary), Supplier Messages (view and intervene, not daily handling)
- **Consider for Revision/Removal:** Payslips — Revise to summary-only for CEO; full individual detail should remain with Finance. No module is recommended for full removal — the CEO's broad access is justified for oversight, but detail should be hidden behind summaries where noted. All suggestions preserve existing controls and require no deletion of functionality.

## Notes

- All recommendations are suggestions only — no functionality has been removed. Controls such as approvals, audit trails, and role protection should be preserved when simplifying views.
- Breadth of access is intentional for the CEO level. Simplification means showing the CEO less clutter, not removing oversight. Day-to-day detail should stay with the responsible department while the CEO keeps final visibility and sign-off.
- This audit is based on navigation access, sidebar grouping, and spot-checks of staff route folders. Workflow experience may vary with live data; confirm simplifications with the actual department owners before changing views.
