# Objective
## Enforce Role-Based Access Control and Least-Privilege Permissions in Supabase

---

## Role

You are an access-control and role-permission reviewer with expertise in Supabase authorization, role-based access control, and least-privilege permission design. Approach this objective by aligning system permissions with the actual responsibilities assigned to each user role.

---

## Description

Review the current codebase, documentation, and Supabase access controls to ensure that each system role has only the permissions required to perform its assigned responsibilities. Although the CEO serves as the company administrator and may retain visibility across all system pages, this authority should not automatically grant unrestricted operational permissions to create, read, update, or delete every type of record. The CEO role should primarily support company-wide oversight, monitoring, reviewing requests, and approving or rejecting actions, while operational responsibilities should remain assigned to the appropriate roles, such as accountants, managers, suppliers, mechanics, and other users. The resulting permission structure should separate executive authority from operational responsibilities and prevent users from performing actions outside the scope of their assigned roles.

---

## Primary Objective

Audit and refine Supabase access controls and role-level permissions so that every user can access and perform only the actions that correspond to the responsibilities of their assigned role.

---

## Secondary Objectives

- Preserve the CEO's company-wide visibility and executive authority without automatically granting permission to perform every operational task in the system.
- Distribute operational permissions among the user roles responsible for performing those specific processes and workflows.
- Prevent users from creating, modifying, deleting, or otherwise managing records that fall outside their assigned responsibilities.
- Ensure application behavior, documented role responsibilities, and Supabase authorization rules are aligned.

---

## Success Criteria

- Each user role is limited to the pages, records, processes, and actions required for its documented responsibilities.
- The CEO can review, monitor, approve, and reject relevant company activities without unnecessarily receiving unrestricted operational CRUD permissions.
- Operational CRUD permissions are assigned to the roles responsible for the corresponding workflows.
- A mechanic can access mechanical inspections and assigned vehicle-related tasks but cannot create a new vehicle when vehicle creation belongs to another role.
- Supabase access controls consistently enforce the intended role-level restrictions rather than relying only on application-interface limitations.

---

## Constraints

- Do not assume that the role with the highest organizational authority requires permission to perform every operational function.
- Permissions must be based on role responsibilities rather than organizational rank alone.
- Users must not receive access to actions or workflows unrelated to their assigned responsibilities.
- CEO access to all system pages may remain because the CEO acts as the company administrator, but page visibility should be treated separately from operational action permissions.

---

## Context & Dependencies

- The current codebase and documentation already define or describe the system's user roles and responsibilities.
- The CEO currently has access to all pages because the CEO functions as the company administrator.
- Supabase is responsible for enforcing system access controls and role-level permissions.
- Existing roles include the CEO, accountants, managers, suppliers, mechanics, and other operational users.
- Permission decisions should be aligned with the responsibilities already established for these roles.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Separation of Visibility and Operational Authority

Treat access to system pages separately from permission to perform actions within those pages. The CEO may retain visibility across the system because the role serves as the company administrator, but that visibility should not automatically provide unrestricted authority to create, update, or delete all underlying records. Operational permissions should instead be granted according to the responsibilities assigned to each role.

### CEO Permission Model

The CEO may hold the highest organizational authority without functioning as every operational role in the system. The role should primarily support company-wide monitoring, review, approval, and rejection of relevant requests and activities. Operational actions that belong to accountants, managers, suppliers, mechanics, or other specialized roles should remain assigned to those users unless the existing role responsibilities explicitly require CEO involvement.

### Responsibility-Based Permissions

Access should be determined by what a user is responsible for performing rather than by organizational seniority alone. Each role should receive the minimum set of permissions necessary to complete its assigned processes and workflows. Users should not be able to perform unrelated actions simply because they can view the corresponding page or record.

### Mechanic Access Example

A mechanic should be able to access mechanical inspections and vehicle-related tasks assigned to the mechanic role. The mechanic should not be permitted to create a new vehicle when vehicle creation belongs to another user role. Supabase authorization should enforce this distinction so that the restriction remains effective even if an unauthorized action is attempted outside the normal application interface.

### Supabase Permission Verification

Review the existing Supabase authorization configuration against the documented responsibilities of every role. Confirm that database-level permissions allow required actions while denying actions outside the user's responsibility. Any overly broad permissions should be identified and refined so that the final authorization model consistently follows role-based, responsibility-specific access.
